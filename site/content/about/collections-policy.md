## Mission {#mission}

The mission of the Ace Archive project is to document and preserve the histories
of queer communities whose histories are underdocumented, with a particular
focus on communities in the asexual and aromantic spectrum.

## Values {#values}

- **Dignity**: Archival work is inherently subjective. When interpreting and
labeling materials, the collection should affirm identity and honor
self-determination.
- **Justice**: Archival work is inherently political. The project should uplift
marginalized voices and challenge harmful power structures and social
hierarchies.
- **Durability**: The project should be resilient to changes in funding,
technology, infrastructure, and maintainership. The collection should outlast
the project.
- **Accessibility**: The collection should be made available and accessible to
as many people as possible. Barriers to access are a social problem, and we lead
with the belief that they can be overcome.
- **Transparency**: Transparency in decision-making builds community trust.
Transparency is prerequisite to accountability.
- **Community**: We can only effect radical change by standing together.
Building community is not in service of the mission; it is the mission.

## Purpose {#purpose}

Asexual and aromantic history are underdocumented; historical materials relevant
to the history of this community are rarely labeled as such in archival
collections.

Much of asexual and aromantic history is documented informally through blogs,
message boards, social media posts, and other similar media. This is a fragile
situation; when a site goes down or an account is deleted, this history is lost.

The purpose of Ace Archive is twofold:

1. Consolidate and label historical materials to make them easier to find and
   access.
2. Give materials in danger of becoming lost media a stable home on the web.

## Scope {#scope}

Ace Archive is a digital archive; it does not accept physical materials.
Consider donating physical materials to archives better equipped to process them
and submitting digitized versions to Ace Archive.

Materials in the collection should relate to the asexual, aromantic, or adjacent
communities in some way. This includes related topics like celibacy and the many
historical labels that predate contemporary definitions of "asexual" and
"aromantic".

Materials in the collection should have some degree of historical relevance.
This is a highly subjective designation, but self-published materials are less
likely to be accepted.

The collection is not strictly limited to a specific timeframe. That being said,
the historical relevance of a piece of media is often not immediately apparent,
so highly recent materials may not be accepted. Links to tertiary sources like
historical retrospectives, timelines, and other archival collections are an
exception.

Currently, the collection is primarily focused on queer history in the United
States. This comes from a lack of expertise and cultural context on the part of
the project maintainers and does not reflect the intended scope of the project.
Materials from other geographic and cultural contexts are welcome.

Currently, the collection consists primarily of English-language materials.
Materials in other languages are welcome—with or without translation—as long as
sufficient context is provided for the maintainers to assess them.
