## Mission {#mission}

The mission of the Ace Archive project is to document and preserve the histories
of queer communities whose histories are underdocumented, with a particular
focus on communities in the asexual and aromantic spectrum.

## Values {#values}

### Equitable {#equitable}

Equity means affirming identity, honoring self-determination, uplifting
marginalized voices, and challenging harmful power structures and social
hierarchies.

### Durable {#durable}

The project should be resilient to changes in funding, technology,
infrastructure, and maintainership. The collection should outlast the project.

### Accessible {#accessible}

The collection should be made available and accessible to as many people as
possible. Barriers to access are a social problem, and we lead with the belief
that they can be overcome.

### Transparent {#transparent}

Transparency in decision-making builds community trust. Transparency is
prerequisite to accountability.

### Collaborative {#collaborative}

We can only effect radical change by standing together. Building community is
not in service of the mission; it is the mission.

## Scope {#scope}

Ace Archive is a digital archive; it does not accept physical materials.
Consider donating physical materials to archival projects better equipped to
process them and submitting digitized versions to Ace Archive.

Materials in the collection should relate to the asexual, aromantic, or adjacent
communities in some way. This includes related topics like celibacy and the many
historical labels that predate contemporary definitions of "asexual" and
"aromantic".

Materials in the collection should have some degree of historical relevance.
This is a highly subjective designation, but self-published materials are less
likely to be accepted.

This collection is not strictly limited to a specific timeframe. That being
said, the historical relevance of a piece of media is often not immediately
apparent, so highly recent materials may not be accepted. Links to tertiary
sources like historical retrospectives, timelines, and other archival
collections are an exception.

Currently, the collection is primarily focused on queer history in the United
States. This comes from a lack of expertise and cultural context on the part of
the project maintainers and does not reflect the intended scope of the project.
Materials from other geographic and cultural contexts are welcome.

Currently, the collection consists primarily of English-language materials.
Materials in other languages are welcome—with or without translation—as long as
sufficient context is provided for the maintainers to assess them.
